Who I am

I don't wait for a job description to tell me what needs doing.

I started on the help desk fixing tickets. Then I started fixing the system behind them — the reports nobody wanted to run by hand, the new hires nobody had time to onboard, the workload imbalance nobody had named out loud. That instinct is why I was one of ten people chosen from more than two hundred Accenture associates and moved into a People Lead role, with a performance bonus and formal recognition for stabilizing a team under pressure.

Today I run day-to-day operations for a ten-person SAP Commerce Cloud team: prioritizing work, triaging defects against SLA targets, keeping client stakeholders aligned in daily and monthly meetings, and coaching a counselee through monthly 1:1s. When I saw early signs of burnout — two of ten teammates flagged it in catchups — I didn't escalate it and move on. I built a peer-pairing program that cut new-hire ramp-up by at least a week and kept the team steady.

I build the automations that give people their time back, too: Power Automate, n8n, and Groovy pipelines that remove ten to thirty minutes of manual work from every reporting task and hand stakeholders numbers they can trust. Right now I'm partnering with my team lead on a formal path toward Team Lead — taking on more coordination, more stakeholder communication, and more of the work that decides whether a team performs.

Most "impossible" problems are just problems nobody has mapped yet. Mapping them is what I do.

How I work

  • Own the outcome. I follow a problem to its root, not to the edge of my ticket.
  • Stabilize first. Under pressure, people need clarity and fairness before anything else.
  • Automate the repeat. If a task is manual and recurring, it's a candidate for removal.
  • Say the quiet part. Workload imbalance and burnout get named early, in the open.
  • Grow the next person. A team that levels up is worth more than any single firefight.
